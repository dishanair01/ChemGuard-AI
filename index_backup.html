Scientific interpretation

The available adsorption records concern CO₂, H₂, N₂, CH₄, krypton and xenon. These measurements do not establish pharmaceutical adsorption performance.

Pharmaceutical-specific adsorption requires independent experimental evidence, including compound identity, water chemistry, pH, concentration and adsorption conditions.

Public information

Medicine waste & the environment

Simple, non-technical information for people who want to understand why medicine disposal matters.

Why does it matter?

Unused medicines can enter waste and wastewater pathways if discarded improperly. Some pharmaceutical residues have been measured in wastewater, surface water, groundwater and other environmental compartments.

What should the public do?

Follow local municipal, pharmacy or health-authority take-back and disposal instructions. Do not assume that every medicine can be disposed of in the same way.

Important distinction

This platform provides environmental education only. It does not advise anyone to stop, change or discard a prescribed medicine. For medicine-specific disposal questions, ask a pharmacist or local health authority.

The project will add location-specific collection guidance only when an official source confirms the service and its current availability.
